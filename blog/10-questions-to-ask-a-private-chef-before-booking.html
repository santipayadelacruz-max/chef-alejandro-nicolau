10 Questions to Ask a Private Chef Before Booking

A short conversation before booking tells you almost everything you need to know about whether a private chef is the right fit for your event. Here are the ten questions worth asking.

011. Can you tailor the menu to our preferences?

A good private chef designs the menu around you, rather than offering a single fixed set menu. Ask to see examples of past menus, like those on the Menus page, as a starting point for the conversation.

022. How do you handle allergies and dietary requirements?

This should be a normal part of the planning process, not an awkward add-on. Ask how substitutions are handled without compromising the rest of the table's menu.

033. What's included in your quote?

Ingredients, cooking, service and clean-up should be clear from the outset, along with anything charged separately such as staff or bar service.

044. Where do you source your ingredients?

This tells you a lot about quality and consistency — a chef who can speak specifically about their suppliers is usually one who takes sourcing seriously.

055. Have you cooked for events like ours before?

Whether it's a gala, corporate dinner, or yacht event, relevant experience matters. Ask for specifics, not just a general yes.

066. What happens if our guest count changes?

Understanding the flexibility (and any limits to it) avoids awkward conversations closer to the date.

077. Do you bring your own equipment?

Relevant especially for unusual venues, marquees, or yacht galleys where kitchen setups vary.

088. How far in advance do we need to confirm the menu?

This affects how much flexibility you have to adjust based on the season or last-minute preferences.

099. Can you also handle drinks and bar service?

Some private chefs, including Alejandro, offer a full cocktail and bar service alongside the food — useful if you want one point of contact for the whole evening.

1010. What happens with clean-up afterwards?

A private chef service should leave your kitchen as they found it — confirm this is included before the night of the event.

11Reading the Answers, Not Just Asking the Questions

The questions themselves matter less than how they're answered. A chef who welcomes detailed questions, answers specifically rather than vaguely, and proactively raises things you hadn't thought to ask is usually the safer choice — regardless of price point. Vague or evasive answers to any of the questions above are a much stronger warning sign than a slightly higher quote. Trust the quality of the conversation as much as the content of the answers when making your final decision.

12Keeping a Simple Record of the Answers

Once you've asked your questions, it's worth jotting down the key answers somewhere — especially anything about pricing, inclusions or allergy handling. This isn't about catching anyone out; it simply gives you a clear reference point to return to if anything needs clarifying as the event date approaches.

13A Final Thought

Asking these questions isn't about being difficult — it's simply due diligence for a service happening in your own home. A good private chef will welcome the thoroughness, because it means you're both starting the relationship with clear, shared expectations.

14What good answers sound like

A reliable chef answers clearly and without hesitation about experience, menu flexibility, dietary handling, equipment and what is included. They explain how shopping and clean-up work and describe what happens if plans change. Vague responses or reluctance to share details are worth noticing. Ideally, you come away understanding exactly what will happen on the day, who will be in your kitchen and how to contact the chef if something comes up beforehand.

Ask any of these questions directly through the enquiry form, alongside your event details, and you'll get straightforward answers before you commit to anything.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it normal to ask this many questions before booking?

Yes. A private chef is a personal, in-home service, so any experienced chef expects and welcomes detailed questions before booking as part of getting the event right.

Should I ask for references or reviews?

Yes, it is a reasonable request. Genuine client feedback, like the verified reviews on Alejandro Nicolau's Reviews page, gives a realistic sense of what to expect on the night.
