Burns Night Supper at Home with a Private Chef

Key takeaways

  • The date is 25 January. Burns Night marks the birthday of Robert Burns, and many hosts choose the nearest weekend.
  • The order is familiar. Arrival, the Selkirk Grace, soup, the haggis and the Address, the main course, dessert and toasts.
  • The menu is short and generous. Haggis, neeps and tatties, with cullen skink to start and cranachan to finish.

A Burns Night supper at home is a dinner held on or around 25 January to celebrate the Scottish poet Robert Burns, built around haggis, neeps and tatties, a few toasts and a dram of whisky. With a private chef, the host can focus on the evening itself while the cooking, plating and service are handled for you.

Burns suppers have a loose but familiar order, and a good menu respects it while still feeling like a proper dinner party. This guide sets out the running order, the traditional dishes and some ways to modernise them.

01What happens at a Burns Night supper?

Burns Night marks the birthday of Robert Burns, born on 25 January 1759. Suppers vary from family dinners to large formal events, but most follow a similar order.

  1. Welcome and arrival drinks. Guests gather, often with a dram of whisky.
  2. The Selkirk Grace. A short grace recited before the meal.
  3. The starter. Traditionally a soup, such as cullen skink or Scotch broth.
  4. The haggis. It is piped or carried in, and the host recites the Address to a Haggis before it is cut.
  5. The main course. Haggis with neeps and tatties, served with a whisky sauce if you like.
  6. Dessert. Often cranachan, or clootie dumpling.
  7. Toasts and readings. The Immortal Memory, a toast to the lassies and a reply, with songs or poems between courses.

A summary of the tradition is on Wikipedia's page on the Burns supper.

02What do you serve at a traditional Burns supper?

The classic menu is short and generous. Haggis is the centre of it, served with mashed swede (neeps) and mashed potato (tatties). A soup comes first, usually cullen skink, a smoked haddock, potato and onion soup, or Scotch broth. Dessert is typically cranachan, a mix of whipped cream, toasted oats, raspberries, honey and whisky, or clootie dumpling, a spiced steamed pudding.

A cheeseboard with oatcakes often follows. Whisky is served throughout, and many hosts choose a small number of drams rather than a long list.

03How can a private chef modernise a Burns supper?

Tradition does not have to mean a heavy or dated menu. A chef can keep the dishes everyone expects while lifting the execution and the plating, and a few ideas show how that can work.

  • Haggis in a lighter form. Small haggis croquetas or bon-bons as a canapé, so the dish appears in the evening without taking over the table.
  • A refined cullen skink. Served in small cups as a first course, with smoked haddock and a drizzle of herb oil.
  • Neeps and tatties, reworked. A silky swede purée and crisp potato, both properly seasoned.
  • Cranachan, lighter. Layered in glasses, with seasonal fruit when raspberries are not at their best in January.
  • A Scottish cheese course. Served with oatcakes and a chutney.

Alejandro's training in San Sebastián and his Mediterranean cooking bring technique and balance to these dishes, and the menu stays recognisably Scottish. You can browse the style of his cooking on the sample menus.

04Can a Burns supper be vegetarian or plant-based?

Yes. Vegetarian haggis is widely available and works well with neeps and tatties and a mushroom or whisky-and-peppercorn sauce. Cullen skink can be replaced with a leek and potato soup or a roasted root vegetable soup, and cranachan adapts to a plant-based cream and oat mixture.

For guests with allergies or other requirements, the same rules apply as for any private dinner: share them early. Our guide to dietary requirements and allergies explains the process, and the chef cooks plant-based menus as well as halal-friendly and kosher-style menus, which are not certified.

05What about whisky and drinks?

A Burns supper is a good occasion for a small, thoughtful whisky selection: perhaps a lighter dram on arrival and a richer one for the toasts. Many hosts also like a whisky cocktail as a welcome drink for guests who prefer something longer.

If you want a bar for the evening, the bar and cocktail service can be added to the same booking. Our guide to wine and menu pairing explains how drinks and courses fit together.

06How do you run the evening at home?

The main thing to agree in advance is who does what. Choose someone to recite the Address to a Haggis, someone to give the Immortal Memory and a pair of speakers for the toast to the lassies and the reply. Keep each short, because they sit between courses and the food should arrive hot.

The chef plans the timing of every course around those moments. A small piper or recording for the haggis is optional but popular. If you have never hosted a Burns supper before, ask for a simple printed running order for each place setting so guests know what to expect.

07What should you decide before the day?

A handful of decisions make the evening run smoothly, and most of them are quick to settle in the first planning conversation.

  • Guest numbers and seating. The chef plans quantities and the plating timeline from these.
  • Who speaks, and when. The Address to a Haggis, the Immortal Memory and the toasts each need a person and a slot.
  • Dietary needs. Share vegetarian, plant-based and allergy requirements early.
  • Drinks. Choose the whisky, the welcome drink and whether you want a bar.
  • The kitchen. A short check of ovens, fridge space and access avoids surprises on the day.

08Where can a private chef cook a Burns supper?

Burns suppers are held across the UK, and not only in Scotland. In London, the team can cook at home for a family or a group of friends. In Scotland, the same service is available in Edinburgh, Glasgow, Perthshire and the Scottish Highlands, where Alejandro and his team travel to lodges and houses.

The date matters: 25 January is a popular evening, and many hosts choose the nearest weekend. Book early using our guide on how far in advance to book a private chef.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Burns Night?

Burns Night is on 25 January, the birthday of Robert Burns, who was born in 1759. Many hosts hold the supper on the nearest weekend.

What do you eat at a Burns supper?

The traditional meal is haggis with neeps (mashed swede) and tatties (mashed potato), often with cullen skink or Scotch broth to start and cranachan or clootie dumpling for dessert.

Can a Burns supper be vegetarian?

Yes. Vegetarian haggis is widely available, soup can be made with leeks or root vegetables, and cranachan can be adapted to a plant-based version.

Do I need to recite poetry at a Burns supper?

It is traditional but not essential. Most suppers include the Address to a Haggis and a few toasts; readings and songs are optional and can be kept short.

Can a private chef cook a Burns supper in London?

Yes. The team cooks at home in London and travels to Scotland and elsewhere in the UK, handling the cooking, plating and service so the host can enjoy the evening.
