Corporate Christmas Party Ideas for Teams in London

By Alejandro Nicolau, Private Chef·Published 7 Nov 2026·Updated 7 Nov 2026

Key takeaways

  • Choose the format first. Seated dinner, canapé reception, sharing lunch or walking dinner: each suits a different team size and mood.
  • Keep it short and well paced. Two to three hours with food arriving at a steady rhythm beats a long evening with a gap in the middle.
  • Add one signature detail. A welcome cocktail, a carved dish or a dessert table gives people something to talk about.
  • Plan for every diet. Collect dietary needs with the invitation, and make the vegetarian dish a proper one.
  • Book as early as you can. Weekday evenings in the first three weeks of December go first.

The best corporate Christmas party ideas start with the format, not the venue: a seated dinner, a canapé reception, a sharing-table lunch or a walking dinner. Pick the format for your team size and time available, then add one memorable detail, such as a welcome cocktail or a chef-led dish, and book early.

Alejandro Nicolau, a London private chef trained in San Sebastian under Martin Berasategui, cooks for company parties in offices, private dining rooms and venues across London. The ideas below come from what actually works on the day, with timings and logistics rather than slogans.

01What are the best corporate Christmas party formats?

Most company parties fall into four formats, and the choice shapes everything else: the space, the staffing, the budget of time and the feel of the evening.

  • Seated dinner. Three courses or a shared-plate menu around one or two tables. Best for 8 to 60 people and for client or leadership dinners.
  • Canapé reception. Standing, mingling, easy to scale. Best for larger teams or when the party is also a networking evening.
  • Sharing-table lunch. Platters and bowls passed along the table, often in the office. Best for a daytime celebration that does not eat into the evening.
  • Walking dinner. Several small courses served in stages while guests move around. Best when you want the feel of a dinner without everyone being seated.

If you are unsure, ask what the team would enjoy most on an ordinary evening. A relaxed team usually prefers sharing food and movement; a formal client group usually prefers a seated table. The corporate Christmas party catering page sets out how each format is delivered.

02How do you choose the right Christmas party format for your team?

Three questions settle it quickly. How many people are coming, how long can the party run, and what do guests want to do besides eat?

  • Under 20 guests: a seated dinner or a chef-led sharing menu works well, with room for conversation across the whole table.
  • 20 to 60 guests: a canapé reception followed by a seated or walking dinner, or a longer reception with substantial pieces.
  • 60 guests and more: stations, tray-passed canapés and a clear service plan, with the team and bar sized to the room.

Mixed-seniority teams often enjoy a format that mixes people up, such as canapés and standing food, because it avoids the fixed seating plan that can feel like a meeting. Client events lean the other way: a seated table gives you the time to talk.

03What food works best at a corporate Christmas party?

Festive does not have to mean heavy. The most successful company parties offer seasonal flavours with enough lightness that guests are comfortable after three hours.

  • To start: a velouté of roasted squash or chestnut, a cured fish plate or a citrus-dressed salad with winter leaves.
  • Main: roast turkey crown or a slow-cooked beef dish with proper gravy, with a vegetarian main of equal ambition, such as a wild mushroom and chestnut pithivier.
  • Sides: roasted roots, sprouts with chestnuts, potatoes cooked in good fat and a bright, acidic element to cut through the richness.
  • To finish: a chocolate and orange dessert, a spiced pear dish or a mixed dessert table for a standing event.

A Spanish touch also works well in December: jamón and croquetas as canapés, a seafood course and turrón-style desserts. It is a lighter, more interesting alternative to a pure British Christmas dinner. For a canapé-led evening see corporate canapé catering.

04How do you add a memorable detail without overcomplicating the party?

One thing done well is better than five done adequately. Pick a single feature and let the rest of the evening support it.

  • A welcome cocktail on arrival, with an alcohol-free version alongside, so everyone starts with something in hand. See bar and cocktail hire.
  • A chef-carved dish at the table or station, which gives the room a focal point.
  • A dessert table with mini tarts, chocolate and fruit, which keeps people mingling after the main course.
  • A team cooking class before dinner for a smaller group, which doubles as the activity. See team building cooking classes.

Avoid anything that depends on guests being comfortable performing in front of colleagues. Food and drink are the safest shared experience.

05How long should a corporate Christmas party last?

Two to three hours is the sweet spot for a food-led party. That gives time for a drink on arrival, a proper meal and some conversation without energy dropping.

  1. 0:00 to 0:45: arrival, welcome drink and canapés.
  2. 0:45 to 2:00: seated courses or the main food service.
  3. 2:00 to 2:30: dessert, coffee and short speeches, if any.
  4. After 2:30: the party is free-flowing; keep a dessert table and drinks going.

Keep speeches short and place them between courses rather than at the end, when attention has gone. If the party is in an office, agree the finishing time with building management in advance.

06How do you cater for different diets at a company party?

In a team of any size, several people will need something different. Collect dietary requirements with the invitation, not on the day, and give the chef the list a week ahead.

Vegetarian and vegan dishes should be proper plates, not the side dishes of the main menu. Halal-friendly, kosher-style, gluten-free and allergy needs can all be planned for, although cross-contact in a shared kitchen can never be excluded entirely, so anyone with a severe allergy should be discussed before booking. See private chef dietary requirements for how this is handled.

07When should you book a corporate Christmas party caterer?

As early as the date is confirmed. The most popular slots are weekday evenings and Thursdays and Fridays in the first three weeks of December, and caterers fill those first.

  • Choose two or three possible dates rather than one.
  • Confirm guest numbers within about ten per cent, and the final number a week before.
  • Share the venue details: kitchen access, lift and loading, and finish time.
  • Ask for a written proposal with menu, staffing and timings.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What are some good corporate Christmas party ideas for a small team?

For under 20 people, a chef-led seated dinner or sharing-table lunch works best, with a welcome cocktail and a single standout dish. A cooking class before dinner adds an activity without the cost of a large event.

How long should a company Christmas party last?

Two to three hours is ideal for a food-led party: about 45 minutes for drinks and canapés, an hour or more for the meal, then dessert and coffee. Longer than that and energy usually dips unless the format changes.

Can a private chef cater an office Christmas party?

Yes. Alejandro Nicolau and his team cook in office kitchens, private dining rooms and venues across London, bringing equipment and staff, and plan around building access and timings in advance.

When should I book Christmas party catering in London?

As soon as you have a date. Weekday evenings in the first three weeks of December are the most requested and fill first, so enquire early and offer two or three alternative dates.

How do I cater for dietary needs at a work party?

Collect requirements with the invitation and share them with the chef about a week ahead. Vegetarian, vegan, halal-friendly and gluten-free menus can be planned, though cross-contact cannot be excluded entirely.
