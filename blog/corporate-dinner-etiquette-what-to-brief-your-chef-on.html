A corporate dinner carries a different kind of pressure than a personal celebration — it's an extension of your company's image, often in front of clients, investors or senior colleagues. Briefing your chef properly makes a real difference.

01Share the purpose of the evening

A relationship-building client dinner, an internal celebration, or a formal investor evening each call for a slightly different tone — sharing this context helps shape a menu and service style that fits the occasion.

02Flag any cultural or dietary considerations early

International guests may have specific dietary practices or cultural preferences worth knowing in advance, so the menu can be designed with genuine consideration rather than a last-minute adjustment.

03Clarify the formality level

A boardroom-style dinner and a more relaxed after-work gathering call for different menu formats — a formal seated multi-course dinner versus a more casual shared-plate style. Being clear about this upfront avoids a mismatch on the night.

04Consider timing around the business agenda

If the dinner includes a presentation, speeches, or planned discussion time, sharing this schedule helps the kitchen and service team pace the meal so food doesn't interrupt (or get interrupted by) the business side of the evening.

05Think about discretion

For sensitive business conversations happening over dinner, a private, professional service — rather than a busy restaurant setting — offers a level of discretion worth considering, something covered further on the Corporate Events section of the Events page.

06Why This Reflects on Your Company as Much as the Occasion Itself

For many guests, a corporate dinner is their most personal interaction with your company outside of formal meetings — which makes the quality and thoughtfulness of the evening a genuine extension of your brand. A well-briefed chef, working from clear context about your guests and the purpose of the evening, helps ensure that impression is a considered, positive one rather than a generic, forgettable dinner.

07Considering the Broader Guest Experience

Beyond the meal itself, small touches — a menu card reflecting your company's branding subtly, or a dish named to reference something meaningful to the business — can reinforce the professional, considered impression a corporate dinner is meant to create.

08An Evening That Reflects Well on You

A properly briefed private chef turns a corporate dinner from a logistical obligation into a genuine asset for your business relationships — worth the extra planning time upfront.

09Considering the Full Guest List, Not Just the Host Company

For dinners involving multiple organisations, it's worth thinking beyond your own team's preferences to the guests you're hosting as well — their likely dietary needs, cultural considerations, and general expectations for the evening. A menu that thoughtfully considers everyone at the table reflects well on the hosting organisation specifically.

10One Final Consideration

If the event is being expensed or reported internally, having a clear, itemised invoice ready afterwards is worth confirming in advance — a small administrative detail that's easy to overlook amid the bigger planning decisions.

11Worth the Preparation

A little extra preparation before a corporate dinner consistently pays off in how smoothly, and how professionally, the evening actually unfolds.

12Practical points for a business evening

Share the schedule, including any speeches or presentations, so courses can be timed around them. Mention the seniority of attendees and any guests who might need extra discretion, and confirm whether staff will remain in the room during discussions. Dietary requirements and cultural considerations should be collected early, as business guests often have precise needs. Finally, agree how invoicing and confirmations will be handled so the administrative side runs as smoothly as the dinner itself.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a private chef accommodate an international client list with varied dietary practices?

Yes. Sharing guest details and any known preferences in advance lets a private chef design the menu thoughtfully around an international client list with varied dietary practices.

Is a private chef dinner more discreet than a restaurant booking for sensitive business conversations?

Generally yes. A private dinner removes the unpredictability of a public restaurant, which matters for confidential business conversations.
