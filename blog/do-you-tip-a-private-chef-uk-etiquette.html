Do You Tip a Private Chef? UK Etiquette Explained

By Alejandro Nicolau, Private Chef·Published 1 Oct 2026·Updated 11 Oct 2026

It's one of the most commonly searched, rarely asked questions about hiring a private chef: is a tip expected, and if so, how much? Here's a straightforward answer.

Tipping isn't required, but it's appreciated

Unlike a restaurant, where a service charge is often built into the bill, a private chef's quote is usually the full cost of the service — a tip is a discretionary way to recognise exceptional service, not an obligation baked into the price.

When hosts typically tip

Tipping tends to happen when a chef has gone well beyond the brief — handling a last-minute guest addition gracefully, adapting to a dietary change on the day, or simply delivering an evening that exceeded expectations. It's entirely at your discretion.

How much is reasonable?

There's no fixed percentage in UK private chef culture the way there is in some restaurant service norms. Some hosts round up to a convenient number, others add a modest percentage of the total. What matters more than the amount is that it's a genuine gesture, not an expectation to worry about.

What about staff, not just the chef?

If your event includes wait staff or a bartender alongside the chef, it's worth considering whether a tip is intended for the whole team or the chef specifically — most hosts simply hand it to the chef to distribute, but you can also do this directly if you prefer.

The bottom line

Don't let uncertainty about tipping affect your decision to hire a private chef. It's a nice-to-have, not a hidden cost, and any experienced chef will never make it feel awkward either way.

How This Compares to Other Personal Services

It helps to think of tipping a private chef the same way you might think about tipping a hairdresser who comes to your home, or a photographer at a private event — a discretionary thank-you for personal, in-home service, rather than a fixed industry expectation like restaurant service charges. Because there's no shared bill to add a percentage to, the decision naturally feels more personal, and most hosts find that once they've enjoyed the evening, the question of whether or how much to tip answers itself.

A Simple Way to Decide in the Moment

If you find yourself genuinely unsure on the night, a reasonable approach is to ask yourself whether the evening exceeded what you expected going in. If the answer is yes, a modest tip is a fitting way to acknowledge that — and if you're still unsure, it's perfectly fine to simply express your thanks warmly instead.

Keep It Simple

At the end of the day, tipping is a small, optional part of a much bigger evening. Don't let uncertainty about etiquette distract from planning the parts of the event that actually matter — the menu, the guests, and the occasion itself.

A Note for International Guests

For hosts or guests more familiar with tipping cultures in other countries, it's worth knowing that UK private dining etiquette is generally more relaxed and less percentage-driven than, for example, US restaurant culture. A genuine thank you, with or without a tip, is always well received, and there's no need to feel obligated by norms from a different country's dining culture.

Practical points on tipping

If you decide to tip, doing so at the end of the evening is straightforward and appreciated, especially after a smooth event. Many hosts base the gesture on the complexity of the menu, the size of the team and how well the evening ran. A tip is never expected as part of the quote, and a kind word or a written review is also a valued way of saying thank you.
