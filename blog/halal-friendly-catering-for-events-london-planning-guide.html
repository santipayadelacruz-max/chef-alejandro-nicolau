Halal-Friendly Catering in London: Event Planning Guide

Key takeaways

  • It is a menu approach, not a certification. Halal-friendly means the menu is planned to halal requirements; it does not mean certified.
  • Plan the whole menu. No pork or alcohol in any dish, including sauces, stocks and desserts.

Halal-friendly catering for an event means planning the whole menu around halal requirements: halal-sourced meat, no pork or alcohol in cooking, and careful separation in the kitchen. It is a menu approach, not a certification, so share your requirements early and ask the caterer to explain how they handle sourcing, utensils and cross-contact.

Halal-friendly catering is easiest to plan when the whole menu is built around it from the start. Alejandro Nicolau, a London private chef trained under Martin Berasategui, cooks halal-friendly menus for family gatherings, Eid and Ramadan events, weddings and corporate occasions across London. This guide explains what to plan and what to ask.

01What does halal-friendly catering mean?

Halal means permissible under Islamic dietary law. In catering it covers the meat used, how it is sourced and prepared, and the exclusion of pork, alcohol and other non-permitted ingredients, including in stocks, sauces and desserts.

Halal-friendly is the term used here because the team plans menus to halal requirements without claiming certification. If you need a specific certification or a particular standard, say so at the start so the chef can tell you honestly whether it can be met.

02What should you tell your caterer?

  1. What you need. Halal-sourced meat only, no alcohol in cooking, or a stricter standard your family or community follows.
  2. Who the guests are. Mixed groups often need both halal and non-halal options, planned so they do not mix.
  3. The occasion. A wedding, an Eid lunch and an iftar all have different rhythms and expectations.
  4. Allergies and other diets. Halal does not replace other requirements.

See halal-friendly private chef in London for how this is handled for private clients.

03How is a halal-friendly menu planned?

Planning starts with ingredients rather than substitutions. A menu built around grilled fish, slow-cooked lamb, rice dishes, vegetables and rich desserts needs very little adaptation.

  • Meat and poultry: sourced from a supplier that meets your standard, with a clear conversation about what that is.
  • Stocks and sauces: built without alcohol, such as wine, brandy or mirin.
  • Desserts: free of gelatine of non-halal origin and alcohol in flavourings.
  • Drinks: a strong alcohol-free menu, since many guests will not drink alcohol.

Spanish, Mediterranean and Middle Eastern cooking suit halal-friendly menus well, with seafood, vegetables, lamb and grains as the base. See Lebanese private chef and Moroccan private chef for related menus.

04How does the kitchen work?

In a private chef or event setting, the chef controls the kitchen for the day, which makes careful practice easier than in a large shared production kitchen.

  • Dedicated utensils, boards and pans for halal dishes where needed.
  • Separate storage and labelling for ingredients.
  • Clear plating and service so dishes are not mixed.

Even so, cross-contact cannot be fully excluded in any shared kitchen, so guests with strict requirements should speak to the chef before booking.

05What event menus work well?

  • Eid lunch or dinner: a sharing table of grilled meats, rice, vegetables and desserts. See Eid private chef.
  • Iftar: a meal that begins with dates and light dishes, then moves to a fuller main. See Ramadan iftar private chef.
  • Wedding or engagement: a seated menu or reception food planned entirely to your standard.
  • Corporate events: halal-friendly options planned into the whole menu so no one eats separately. See corporate catering.

For mixed groups, make the main menu halal-friendly for everyone, which avoids the need for separate dishes.

06What questions should you ask a caterer?

  1. Where does the meat come from, and does it meet the standard we need?
  2. Is any alcohol used in cooking, sauces or desserts?
  3. How are utensils, surfaces and storage handled?
  4. Can the menu be halal-friendly throughout, not just for one dish?
  5. How do you handle cross-contact and what can you not guarantee?

A good caterer answers plainly and tells you what cannot be guaranteed. Be cautious of any caterer who gives certainty without detail.

07How do you plan drinks for a halal-friendly event?

Drinks matter more than hosts expect, because many guests will not drink alcohol and the drinks menu is often the most visible part of the event.

  • Offer at least three well-made alcohol-free drinks, not only water and juice.
  • Use sparkling drinks, spiced teas, fresh juices and mocktails to give guests variety.
  • Avoid alcohol in cooking, desserts and flavourings.
  • Serve everything in the same glassware so no guest is singled out.

See alcohol-free cocktails for events for ideas that suit any event.

08What about mixed groups and different standards?

Many events include guests with different requirements. The simplest and most respectful approach is to plan the main menu to the strictest standard, so that everyone can eat everything.

  • Ask the host or family which standard applies.
  • Plan the whole menu at that standard rather than making a separate dish for one table.
  • Label dishes clearly for guests with other dietary needs.
  • Tell the chef about any religious or cultural expectations around timing, such as an iftar.

This avoids guests eating separately and makes the event feel shared.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What does halal-friendly catering mean?

It means the menu is planned to halal requirements, with halal-sourced meat and no pork or alcohol in cooking, but without a claim of certification. Share your standard with the caterer so they can say honestly whether it can be met.

Is halal-friendly the same as halal-certified?

No. Halal-friendly describes how a menu is planned, not a certification. If you need certified halal food, tell the caterer at the start so they can explain what they can and cannot provide.

Can a private chef cook a halal menu at home?

Yes. A chef can plan and cook a halal-friendly menu in your kitchen, using sourcing, utensils and preparation that meet your requirements, though cross-contact cannot be fully excluded in any shared kitchen.

What menus work well for halal-friendly events?

Grilled fish, slow-cooked lamb, rice dishes, vegetable dishes and rich desserts need little adaptation. Spanish, Mediterranean and Middle Eastern cooking suit halal-friendly menus well.

Do you need alcohol-free drinks at a halal event?

Yes. Many guests will not drink alcohol, so plan a strong alcohol-free menu with well-made cocktails, juices and sparkling drinks, and avoid alcohol in cooking and desserts.
