Hidden Costs to Ask About Before Booking a Private Chef

Key takeaways

  • Ask what's included in the headline number. A quote should specify exactly what it covers: ingredients, cooking, plating, and clean-up are standard.
  • Ask about travel and parking. If your event is outside central London, or in a venue with limited parking or loading access, ask whether that's already factored into the quote or added afterwards.
  • Ask about minimum spends or guest count minimums. Some private chef services have a minimum guest count or minimum order value.
  • Ask about last-minute changes. Guest numbers change.
  • Ask about equipment and kitchen requirements. For events in a private residence, on a yacht, or in a marquee, ask whether the chef needs specific kitchen equipment supplied, or whether they bring their own.

Most disappointing private chef experiences don't come from bad food — they come from a quote that turns out not to mean what the client thought it meant. Asking the right questions before you book removes almost all of that risk.

01Ask what's included in the headline number

A quote should specify exactly what it covers: ingredients, cooking, plating, and clean-up are standard. Whether travel, wait staff, hire equipment, or a bar service are included — or priced separately — should be spelled out clearly before you agree to anything.

02Ask about travel and parking

If your event is outside central London, or in a venue with limited parking or loading access, ask whether that's already factored into the quote or added afterwards. A transparent chef will raise this themselves during the consultation.

03Ask about minimum spends or guest count minimums

Some private chef services have a minimum guest count or minimum order value. It's worth knowing this upfront, especially for very small, intimate dinners, so there's no mismatch between what you had in mind and what's actually on offer.

04Ask about last-minute changes

Guest numbers change. Dietary requirements come up late. Ask how the chef handles adjustments in the days before your event, and whether that affects the price — a good private chef will be upfront that some flexibility is normal, within reason.

05Ask about equipment and kitchen requirements

For events in a private residence, on a yacht, or in a marquee, ask whether the chef needs specific kitchen equipment supplied, or whether they bring their own. This is especially relevant for yacht events, where galley space and equipment vary enormously from vessel to vessel.

06The simplest way to avoid all of this

Work with a chef who sends a genuinely itemised quote and is happy to talk through every line of it before you commit. That conversation, more than the number itself, tells you whether you're in good hands.

07A Simple Checklist Before You Sign Off

Before confirming any booking, it's worth running through a short mental checklist: does the quote include travel, staff, and equipment hire where relevant; is there a clear deposit and balance structure; and has the chef been upfront about how last-minute changes are handled. If all three of these are clear and comfortable, you're almost certainly dealing with a transparent, professional service. If any of them feel vague or brushed past, it's worth asking again before you commit — a good private chef will never mind being asked to clarify.

08Getting Everything in Writing

Whatever is agreed verbally during a consultation, make sure the final quote or booking confirmation reflects it in writing. This isn't about distrust — it simply ensures both sides have the same clear reference point to work from, which avoids any confusion or mismatched expectations closer to the date of your event.

09Confidence Comes From Clarity

None of these questions are unusual to ask, and a confident, transparent chef will answer all of them without hesitation. That clarity, more than the number itself, is what should give you confidence going into your booking.

10How to compare quotes properly

To compare two proposals fairly, check what each includes: shopping, ingredients, staff, equipment, travel and clean-up. Ask whether service staff or a bartender are part of the quote, and what happens if the guest count changes. A clear written proposal lists these points in plain language. When everything is spelled out, you can judge value rather than being surprised by extras later, and you know exactly what the evening will involve.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I expect a deposit when booking a private chef?

A deposit to secure the date is standard practice for private chefs. It should be explained clearly during booking, with the balance due closer to or on the day of the event.

What happens if my guest count changes after I've booked?

Most private chefs can accommodate reasonable changes to guest numbers with enough notice, though the final quote may adjust, so flag any change to the guest count as early as possible.
