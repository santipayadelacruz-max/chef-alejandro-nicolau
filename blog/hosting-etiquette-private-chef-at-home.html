The Etiquette of Hosting With a Private Chef in Your Home

Hosting with a private chef in your home is a slightly different experience from a restaurant booking, and a little familiarity with the etiquette makes the evening smoother for everyone involved.

01Give the chef space to work

Once the chef arrives and begins setting up, it's generally best to let them work in the kitchen without frequent check-ins — trust the process, and they'll flag anything they need from you directly.

02Brief your guests, if it helps

For guests unfamiliar with having a private chef at a dinner, a brief mention beforehand — that there will be a chef in the kitchen and possibly wait staff serving — helps everyone feel comfortable rather than unsure how to behave.

03Decide in advance whether the chef should be introduced

Some hosts like to introduce the chef briefly to guests, especially for a special dish or course; others prefer a lower-key presence throughout. Either is fine — just worth deciding ahead of time.

04Be upfront about anything unusual about your home

A tricky parking situation, a kitchen that's smaller than average, or specific access instructions are all worth sharing in advance so the chef can plan around them smoothly.

05Trust the timing

A private chef paces courses based on experience reading the table — let them manage timing rather than requesting each course exactly on schedule, and the evening will flow more naturally.

06Thank them, but there's no need to over-manage the evening

The whole point of hiring a private chef is to be a guest at your own event — a warm thank you at the end goes a long way, without needing to manage every detail throughout the night.

07What First-Time Hosts Often Worry About Unnecessarily

Many first-time clients worry about things that, in practice, rarely matter — whether their kitchen is impressive enough, whether they need to entertain the chef, whether they're expected to help. None of this is necessary. An experienced private chef is entirely comfortable working independently in any reasonably equipped kitchen, and the whole point of the service is for you to relax, not to manage or worry about the process.

08What to Do if Something Doesn't Go Quite to Plan

Even with the best planning, small things occasionally shift on the night — a slightly delayed course, a minor adjustment. A relaxed, understanding approach from the host almost always keeps the evening feeling smooth, since guests generally take their cue from how calm the host appears.

09Relax and Enjoy the Evening

Ultimately, the best approach to hosting with a private chef is simply to trust the process and enjoy being a guest at your own event — that's the entire point of the service.

10A Quick Reassurance for Nervous First-Time Hosts

It's completely normal to feel a little unsure the first time you host with a private chef in your home. Most of that uncertainty disappears within the first few minutes of the chef arriving — professional, organised, and clearly focused on making your evening go smoothly rather than needing you to manage anything at all.

11Small courtesies that make the evening easier

A few simple arrangements help everything run smoothly: tell the chef about any pets or access issues before they arrive, make sure there is clear counter space and fridge room, and let your guests know roughly when dinner will be served. During the evening, a quick thank-you to the chef at the end of service is always appreciated. None of this needs to be elaborate. Good hosting with a private chef is mostly about clear communication beforehand so that on the night you can simply enjoy your own party.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I stay in the kitchen while the chef cooks?

It is generally more relaxing for both host and private chef if you enjoy time with your guests while the chef works; the chef will come to you if anything needs your input.

Is it normal for the chef to bring an assistant or wait staff?

For larger dinners, yes. Additional staff help keep a private chef's service smooth, and this is usually agreed while planning the event.
