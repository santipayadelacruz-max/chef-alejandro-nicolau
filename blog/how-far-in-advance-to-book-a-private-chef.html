How Far in Advance Should You Book a Private Chef?

By Alejandro Nicolau, Private Chef·Published 1 Oct 2026·Updated 11 Oct 2026

Timing a private chef booking right means balancing two things: giving the chef enough notice to plan a great menu, and giving yourself enough flexibility to lock in a date before it's taken by someone else.

For a standard dinner party

Two to four weeks' notice is usually comfortable for a dinner party of up to around twelve guests. This gives enough time to agree a menu, confirm dietary requirements, and source any specific ingredients.

For larger events

Corporate dinners, galas, and events with more than twenty guests benefit from six to eight weeks' notice, particularly if staffing, bar service, or a specific venue setup needs to be arranged around the meal.

For peak dates

December, Valentine's week, and the summer wedding and yacht season all see private chef availability book up faster than usual. If your event falls in one of these windows, starting the conversation two to three months ahead protects your preferred date.

For yacht charters

Booking a yacht chef for a Mediterranean season often needs to happen well before the season starts, since availability across a charter's dates depends on coordinating with the wider crew and provisioning schedule.

Can you book last-minute?

Sometimes — it depends entirely on availability. If your event is coming up fast, it's always worth asking rather than assuming it's too late; a straightforward menu with accessible ingredients can often still be arranged with relatively short notice.

What Happens If You Wait Too Long

The main risk of waiting isn't that a private chef service becomes unavailable altogether — it's that your preferred date, or a fully bespoke menu built around specific ingredients, becomes harder to arrange with very short notice. A chef working within a tighter timeframe can still deliver an excellent evening, but the process may be more streamlined out of necessity — fewer rounds of menu refinement, less flexibility on sourcing specific ingredients. None of this needs to worry you if your event is weeks away; it's simply a reason not to leave a firm date to the very last minute.

A Simple Rule of Thumb to Remember

As a general guide: the bigger, more elaborate, or more date-sensitive your event, the further ahead you should start the conversation. For anything else, reaching out as soon as you have a rough idea of your plans is always a safe, low-pressure way to begin, even if the date itself is still a little flexible.

When in Doubt, Just Ask

Timelines are ultimately guidance, not hard rules — availability shifts constantly, and the only way to know for certain is to reach out with your actual date and see what's possible.

A Note on Weekday Versus Weekend Availability

Weekday dates, particularly outside December and peak wedding season, often have more flexible availability than weekend dates, which tend to book up faster across the board. If your event has any flexibility on day of the week, this is worth keeping in mind when planning your timeline.

A Final Word of Encouragement

Whatever stage of planning you're at, there's rarely a wrong time to reach out and ask — worst case, you'll simply get a clear answer on what's realistic for your specific date.

A realistic timeline

For a dinner party, two to four weeks is comfortable. Weddings, milestone events and corporate occasions benefit from two to three months, and festive dates such as Christmas and New Year's Eve fill even earlier. Yacht charters are best arranged once dates are confirmed. If your date is closer than any of these, it is still worth asking, as last-minute availability does occur. Early enquiries simply give you the widest choice of dates and menu options.

Whatever your timeline, the best first step is simply to ask. Share your date through the enquiry form and find out what's possible.
