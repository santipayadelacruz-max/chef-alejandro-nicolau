How Many Canapés Per Person? A Host's Guide for Events

By Alejandro Nicolau, Private Chef·Published 25 Oct 2026·Updated 25 Oct 2026

Key takeaways

  • Before a seated dinner, plan four to six. Four to six canapés per guest for a 30 to 45 minute reception; six to eight if it runs an hour or more.
  • For a canapé-only party, plan twelve to fifteen. Include several substantial pieces so the canapés feel like a meal, and finish with a few sweet ones.
  • Match variety to guest numbers. Four varieties up to 20 guests, five or six for 20 to 50, seven or eight beyond that.
  • Add a ten per cent buffer. Guests multiplied by pieces, divided by varieties, plus about ten per cent of each.
  • Serve on trays. Tray service lets the kitchen set the pace and keeps the numbers close to plan.

How many canapés per person? For a drinks reception before a seated dinner, plan on four to six canapés per guest. For a longer reception, plan on six to eight. If canapés replace dinner entirely, plan on twelve to fifteen per guest over two to three hours. These are starting points: timing, drinks and the guests themselves all move the number.

Getting canapé numbers wrong spoils an evening quietly. Too few and guests are hungry before the first course; too many and nobody wants dinner. The figures below are the usual planning starting points for private events, with notes on when to move away from them.

01How many canapés per person should you plan for?

The honest answer depends on what the canapés are for. A canapé is a one- or two-bite piece, so the real question is how much of the evening's eating you want them to do. Three situations cover most private events.

  • A short reception before a seated dinner (30 to 45 minutes): four to six pieces per guest.
  • A longer reception before a seated dinner (an hour or more): six to eight pieces per guest.
  • A canapé-only party in place of dinner (two to three hours): twelve to fifteen pieces per guest, with some substantial items among them.

Treat these as a first draft. The sections below explain what pushes the figure up or down.

02How many canapés before a seated dinner?

When dinner follows, canapés welcome guests and settle the room; they are not there to fill anyone. Four to six pieces is usually enough for a reception of 30 to 45 minutes. Guests arrive hungry, they are holding a drink and they are talking, so most people take two or three pieces in the first fifteen minutes and then slow down.

Go towards the top of the range if the reception will run past an hour, if dinner may start late, or if guests arrive straight from work. Go towards the bottom if the meal that follows is generous. A multi-course tasting menu already has its first courses to come, so the canapés work best when they are small and precise, in the style you will see on the sample menus.

03How many canapés for a canapé-only party?

A standing party with no dinner is a different calculation, because the canapés are the meal. Twelve to fifteen pieces per guest across two to three hours is a sensible starting point, and for an evening that runs longer it is safer to plan on more than on less.

The mix matters as much as the number. A dozen delicate bites will not feel like dinner, however good they are. Build in several substantial pieces, such as croquetas, small rolls, fork-eating bowls or a hot piece of meat or fish, and release them in waves so there is always something new on the trays. Finish with two or three sweet pieces so the evening has a natural close.

04How many different canapés should you offer?

Variety keeps the trays interesting without making the kitchen's job impossible. As a guide, four varieties suit a small reception of up to 20 guests, five or six suit 20 to 50 guests, and seven or eight suit larger events. Beyond that, extra choice rarely improves the evening and it adds preparation time and plating risk.

To work out how many of each to make, multiply the number of guests by the pieces per guest, divide by the number of varieties, and add a buffer of about ten per cent. For 24 guests at five pieces each, that is 120 pieces; with five varieties, that is 24 of each, plus a few spare of every kind.

05How do you balance hot, cold, vegetarian and sweet?

A good tray list has a clear shape. Start cold and light, bring in one or two hot pieces once guests have settled, and keep sweet pieces for the end. A useful rule is that at least a third of the savoury varieties should be vegetarian, so vegetarian guests are not left with a single option and everyone else wants to try them too.

Allergens belong to the same plan. Every piece should be known and labelled, or the person carrying the tray should be able to answer questions on the spot. If your guest list includes allergies, tell the chef early: our guide to dietary requirements and allergies explains what that planning looks like, and the Food Standards Agency publishes allergen guidance for food businesses.

06How does the way canapés are served change the numbers?

Tray service gives the most control. When canapés are brought round by a waiter or by the chef, the kitchen sets the pace, so the numbers can follow the room: more trays when guests are lively, fewer when dinner is close. A static table of canapés gets raided early, leaves some guests with nothing and makes it hard to hold to the plan.

Staffing follows the guest list. A small reception may need only the chef and one member of the team on trays, while a reception of 40 or more normally needs several people on service, so every group sees a tray every few minutes. It is one of the points settled during planning, alongside the bar; see how many bartenders you need for 50 guests.

07What pushes the number up or down?

  • Time of day. An early-evening reception for guests coming from work needs more than a late one after a long lunch.
  • Drinks and standing. A generous bar and an hour on your feet both make people hungrier, which argues for the upper end of the range.
  • The guests. A younger or sportier crowd tends to eat more; a mixed, formal group often eats less.
  • The menu that follows. A light dinner leaves room for a heavier reception; a seven-course tasting menu does not.
  • Sweet pieces. If dessert canapés are part of the plan, count them in the total rather than adding them on top.

08A simple way to plan it

  1. Decide what the canapés are for: a welcome, a first course or the whole meal.
  2. Fix the length of the reception.
  3. Pick a starting figure from the ranges above.
  4. Multiply by the number of guests and add about ten per cent.
  5. Divide across four to eight varieties, with a vegetarian share.
  6. Share dietary needs and allergies with the chef early.
  7. Agree how the trays will be served and by whom.

A private chef will normally walk through this with you in the first conversation and adjust once the menu is chosen. The same method scales from a house party to a corporate evening or a gala dinner: only the number of trays and the number of hands change.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How many canapés per person for a wedding?

For a drinks reception before the wedding breakfast, plan on four to six pieces per guest if it is short, and six to eight if guests will stand for an hour or more. Go to the top of the range when there is a long gap before sitting down.

How many canapés do I need for 20, 50 or 100 guests?

At five pieces each before a seated dinner, that is about 100 pieces for 20 guests, 250 for 50 and 500 for 100, plus roughly ten per cent spare. For a canapé-only party at twelve pieces each, it is about 240, 600 and 1,200.

How long should canapés be served for?

Before a seated dinner, 30 to 60 minutes is typical, with the last tray out before guests move to the table. For a canapé-only event, plan two to three hours, with the substantial pieces in the middle and the sweet pieces at the end.

Should vegetarian canapés be counted separately?

Include them in the total. Plan at least a third of the savoury varieties as vegetarian, and make each one a proper piece rather than an afterthought.

Can a private chef cater a canapé-only event?

Yes. A canapé-only evening is a common booking for house parties, corporate receptions and celebrations. The chef plans the number, mix and pacing of the pieces with you and the team serves them on trays.
