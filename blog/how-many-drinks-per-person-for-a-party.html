How Many Drinks Per Person for a Party? Bar Planning Guide

By Alejandro Nicolau, Private Chef·Published 7 Nov 2026·Updated 7 Nov 2026

Key takeaways

  • Two in the first hour, one each hour after. Use this as the baseline for most parties, and add a buffer of about ten per cent.
  • Adjust for the event. Weddings, dancing and warm weather push the number up; seated dinners with wine bring it down.
  • Count glasses, not just drinks. Plan glassware for every drink, not just the number of guests.
  • Offer alcohol-free options. A good non-alcoholic choice protects the drinking pace and includes everyone.
  • Bartenders change the maths. Cocktails are slower to make, so staffing matters more than quantity.

Plan on roughly two drinks per guest in the first hour and one drink per guest for each hour after that. A three-hour party therefore needs about four drinks per person, rising to five or six when guests are dancing, the weather is warm or the event is a celebration. Always add a buffer and plenty of alcohol-free options.

Working out how many drinks per person to plan is the part of party planning that most often goes wrong. Alejandro Nicolau, a London private chef trained under Martin Berasategui, works with bartenders at private parties, weddings and corporate events across London. The figures here are planning starting points that the team uses, to be adjusted to your guests and event.

01How many drinks per person should you plan for a party?

The baseline most hosts use is two drinks per guest in the first hour and one per guest in each hour that follows. That makes four drinks for a three-hour party and six for a five-hour one.

The figure is deliberately simple. It works because people drink faster at the start of an event and then settle into a pace. Adjusting it for your guests, the time of day and the format is what turns a rule of thumb into a plan.

02How many drinks per person by length of party?

  • Two-hour drinks reception: about three drinks per guest.
  • Three-hour party: about four drinks per guest.
  • Four-hour evening event: about five drinks per guest.
  • Five hours or more: about six or more, depending on whether there is dancing and a full meal.

Add a buffer of about ten per cent, so that the last half hour does not run dry. It is far easier to return unopened bottles than to run out.

03How do you adjust the number for the type of event?

The same guest list drinks differently depending on what is happening.

  • Seated dinner with wine: plan wine by the bottle, about half a bottle per guest across the meal, plus welcome drinks beforehand.
  • Standing reception: drinks go faster when guests are standing and holding a glass, so lean towards the top of the range.
  • Dancing or a late finish: add a drink or two per guest after the meal.
  • Warm weather: guests drink more, including more water and soft drinks.
  • Corporate events: usually lower, with a shorter, more controlled bar.

See open bar vs limited bar for how bar format changes consumption.

04How many bottles do you need?

Once you have the drink count, convert it to bottles. These are standard serving sizes that the bar team uses for planning.

  • A 75cl bottle of wine pours about five glasses.
  • A bottle of Champagne or sparkling wine pours about six flutes.
  • A 70cl bottle of spirits makes about 25 single measures, or fewer cocktails if each uses a double.
  • Beer and soft drinks are counted by the bottle or can.

Split the total between wine, sparkling, spirits and soft drinks to suit your guests. A mixed crowd typically drinks more wine at dinner and more spirits and beer later in the evening.

05How many drinks per person at a cocktail party?

A cocktail party follows the same baseline, but the making time changes everything. A cocktail takes far longer to prepare than a glass of wine, so the issue is often not quantity but how fast the bar can serve.

A short cocktail menu of three to five drinks helps, as does batching some ingredients in advance. For more on this see how to plan a cocktail menu for a private party and how many bartenders you need for 50 guests.

06Do you need to plan for alcohol-free drinks?

Yes, and generously. A good alcohol-free option makes the evening more inclusive and keeps the pace of the party comfortable.

  • Plan about one alcohol-free drink for every four or five alcoholic drinks, and more at daytime events.
  • Offer at least two proper alcohol-free options, not only water and cola.
  • Provide plenty of still and sparkling water throughout.

See alcohol-free cocktails for events for ideas.

07How do guests and the season change the numbers?

No two guest lists drink the same way. A few questions help you adjust the baseline before you order.

  • Who is coming? A group of friends who rarely see each other drinks more than colleagues at a work party.
  • What time is it? Daytime events use fewer alcoholic drinks and more soft drinks and water.
  • What is the season? Summer parties need more ice, water and long drinks; winter parties lean towards wine, spirits and warm drinks.
  • Is food served? A full meal slows drinking; canapés alone do less to pace it.

When you are unsure, plan towards the top of the range and keep unopened bottles for returns or later use.

08How do you avoid running out of drinks?

Running out is rarely about the total number. It is usually about one product running out early, such as ice, white wine or a mixer.

  • Order ice generously and keep a reserve in a cool box.
  • Hold back a spare case or two of the most popular wine or beer.
  • Check the stock halfway through the evening and top up quietly.
  • Keep still and sparkling water flowing throughout.

A bartender will usually track stock as the evening goes on and warn you in time to act.

09When do you need a bartender?

For fewer than 20 guests and a simple drinks list, a host or a member of the catering team can manage. Beyond that, a bartender protects both the quality and the pace of service.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How many drinks per person for a 3-hour party?

Plan about four drinks per guest: two in the first hour and one in each of the next two hours. Add a ten per cent buffer, and more if guests will be dancing or the weather is warm.

How many bottles of wine do you need for a dinner party?

Plan about half a bottle of wine per guest across a meal, plus a welcome drink. A 75cl bottle pours around five glasses, so ten guests need about five bottles of wine.

How many drinks per person for a cocktail party?

The baseline is the same, two in the first hour and one each hour after, but cocktails take longer to make. A short menu and a bartender matter more than the total.

How much alcohol-free drink should I order?

About one alcohol-free drink for every four or five alcoholic drinks, and more at daytime events. Offer at least two proper options and plenty of still and sparkling water.

Do I need a bartender for a party?

For under 20 guests with a simple menu, probably not. Above that, a bartender keeps service quick and consistent, and handles ice, glassware and clearing so the host can enjoy the party.
