There's no single number that answers "how much does a private chef cost in London" — and anyone who gives you one figure without asking about your event is guessing. The honest answer is that private chef pricing in London is built from a handful of clear variables, and once you understand them, getting an accurate quote takes minutes, not weeks.

01The variables that actually set the price

Every private chef quote in London is shaped by the same core factors: number of guests, number of courses, the ingredients involved (a wagyu-and-caviar tasting menu costs more to source than a seasonal vegetable menu), whether service staff and bar are included, and how far the chef needs to travel. A birthday dinner for six in Chelsea and a forty-guest gala in Mayfair are simply different jobs, and pricing them the same way would be dishonest.

Menu style matters more than most people expect. A five-course tasting menu with a dedicated waiter takes considerably more planning, prep time and kitchen equipment than a relaxed three-course family dinner — and that shows up in the quote, not because of markup, but because of the actual hours and ingredient cost involved.

02What's usually included in a private chef quote

A proper quote from an experienced private chef should be transparent about what it covers: ingredient sourcing, prep and cooking time, travel to your property or venue, plating and presentation, and clean-up afterwards. Some clients also add wait staff, a bartender, or hire of extra equipment — each of which is priced separately so you know exactly what you're paying for.

What should never be a surprise is a "service charge" or unexplained add-on that appears after you've agreed a menu. A chef who works the way Alejandro does will walk you through every element of the quote before you commit to anything.

03Why per-person pricing is misleading

Some catering companies advertise a flat "per person" rate, which sounds simple but rarely reflects reality. A ten-guest dinner with a wagyu course costs more per head than a forty-guest canapé reception with lighter bites — the maths runs in the opposite direction to what a flat rate implies. This is why a proper consultation, rather than a price list, is the only reliable way to understand your actual cost.

04How to get an accurate number for your event

The fastest way to a genuine, itemised quote is to describe your event as specifically as you can: guest count, date, venue, occasion, and any menu style you have in mind — whether that's a Spanish tasting menu, a refined multi-course dinner, or something built around a full bar and cocktail service. From there, a private chef can put together pricing that reflects your event, not a generic template.

05What a Realistic Range Looks Like

While every quote is genuinely built around your event, it helps to have a rough sense of scale before you start. An intimate dinner for four to six guests with a seasonal three-course menu sits at one end of the spectrum; a ten-course tasting menu for twelve, with wine pairing and wait staff, sits considerably higher. Neither is "expensive" or "cheap" in isolation — the number simply reflects the scope of what's being delivered. What matters most is that whatever figure comes back to you is broken down clearly enough that you can see exactly where it comes from, rather than accepting a single total on faith.

06A Note on Comparing Multiple Quotes

If you're gathering quotes from more than one chef, resist the temptation to simply rank them by total price. Ask each one to break the number down the same way, so you're comparing like for like — the same guest count, the same course count, similar ingredient ambition. Two quotes that look wildly different at first glance often turn out to be pricing genuinely different menus, and once you align the scope, the numbers usually make a lot more sense.

07The Takeaway

Ultimately, the fastest and most reliable way to understand your real cost is a direct conversation rather than searching for a universal number online. Every one of the factors above feeds into a single, personalised quote, and a transparent chef will walk through each of them with you happily, before you ever commit to a date.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a private chef more expensive than a restaurant?

Not necessarily. For groups of six or more, a private chef in London often works out comparable to, or less than, a high-end restaurant tasting menu once the whole table's bill, service and travel are counted, and the evening is fully private and tailored.

Do private chefs charge a flat fee or by the hour?

Most private chef bookings in London, including Alejandro Nicolau's, are quoted as a fixed fee for the whole event based on guest count and menu, not an hourly rate, so you know the total upfront.
