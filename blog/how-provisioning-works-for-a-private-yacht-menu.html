How Provisioning Works for a Private Yacht Menu

By Alejandro Nicolau, Private Chef·Published 1 Oct 2026·Updated 11 Oct 2026

Long before a guest sits down to their first meal on board, a yacht chef has already done the hardest part of the job: provisioning. Getting this right is what makes an entire charter's menu possible.

Planning around the itinerary

Provisioning starts with the charter route — which ports the yacht will pass through, how many days between restocking opportunities, and what's realistically available at each stop. A chef plans menus that work with this rhythm, rather than assuming unlimited access to any ingredient at any time.

Sourcing locally where possible

Part of the appeal of a Mediterranean charter is eating what the region does best — local fish, seasonal produce, and regional specialities picked up along the way. A good yacht chef builds this into the menu rather than shipping everything from a single source.

Managing limited storage

Yacht galleys have a fraction of the storage space of a land kitchen, which means provisioning has to be precise — enough to cover the charter comfortably, without excess that takes up scarce fridge and freezer space or goes to waste.

Planning for guest preferences

Provisioning also accounts for anything guests have specifically requested or flagged in advance — favourite ingredients, dietary requirements, or a particular style of cooking — since restocking mid-charter to fix a gap isn't always simple.

Why this matters to you as the client

The quality and freedness of what lands on your plate mid-charter is a direct result of how well provisioning was planned before you ever boarded. It's one of the clearest signs of an experienced yacht chef versus one still learning the discipline.

What Happens When Provisioning Goes Wrong

Poor provisioning shows up quickly on a charter — a menu that suddenly has to change because an ingredient wasn't secured, or a galley running short on basics mid-trip because quantities weren't planned carefully enough. Experienced yacht chefs build in sensible buffers and contingency plans precisely to avoid this, drawing on knowledge of specific ports and their supply chains built up over multiple seasons. It's this kind of practical, hard-won experience — not just cooking talent — that separates a smooth charter from a stressful one.

Working Around Restricted or Remote Locations

Some charter routes pass through more remote stretches of coastline with limited local supply options. In these cases, provisioning further ahead of time, and carrying a slightly larger buffer of shelf-stable ingredients, becomes even more important — another reason experienced route-specific knowledge matters so much in this role.

The Foundation of a Great Charter Menu

Good provisioning rarely gets noticed directly by guests, but its absence is felt immediately in a limited or repetitive menu. It's the unglamorous groundwork that makes everything else on a charter possible.

Building Relationships With Port-Specific Suppliers

Experienced yacht chefs often maintain relationships with specific suppliers across multiple regular charter destinations, built up over several seasons. This local knowledge — knowing exactly which market or supplier to trust in a given port — is one of the most valuable, hard-to-replace assets an experienced yacht chef brings to a charter.

A Final Thought on Trust

Ultimately, good provisioning comes down to trust — trusting a chef's judgement and experience to make the right calls with limited information, far from the usual comforts of a fully stocked land kitchen.

Planning around storage and itinerary

Yacht galleys have limited fridge and freezer space, so provisioning is staged around the itinerary, with perishables bought close to departure and in ports along the route. The chef balances fresh fish and produce against longer-lasting items and keeps spare options in case weather changes the plan. Clear communication with the captain and the guests' preferences early on make the whole process smoother and the menu more reliable.
