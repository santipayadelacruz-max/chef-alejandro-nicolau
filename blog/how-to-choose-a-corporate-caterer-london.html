How to Choose a Corporate Caterer in London: 12 Questions

By Alejandro Nicolau, Private Chef·Published 7 Nov 2026·Updated 7 Nov 2026

Key takeaways

  • Ask who actually cooks. Find out whether the chef you meet is the chef on the day, and whether food is cooked fresh on site.
  • Check building logistics. Lift bookings, loading, security and kitchen access cause more delays than the food does.
  • Insist on a written proposal. Menu, staffing, timings, equipment and what is not included should all be on paper.
  • Test the dietary process. A good caterer asks about diets early and explains how cross-contact is managed.
  • Plan for change. Ask what happens if numbers rise or fall, and by when.

To choose a corporate caterer in London, ask twelve practical questions: who cooks and serves, whether food is made fresh on site, how building access is handled, how dietary needs are managed, what is included in the proposal and what happens if numbers change. The answers show quickly whether a caterer can deliver.

Knowing how to choose a corporate caterer is mostly a matter of asking the right questions early. A corporate event is judged by its smoothest moments, and most problems start before the day: unclear staffing, no plan for the lift, or a menu that cannot flex. Alejandro Nicolau, a London private chef trained under Martin Berasategui, lists below the questions he would ask if he were the client.

01What should you look for in a corporate caterer?

Three things matter more than any website photo: reliability, flexibility and honesty about what is included. A caterer can produce beautiful food and still fail you if the logistics are loose.

The questions below are grouped into the food, the team, the building and the paperwork. Together they take about twenty minutes to ask, and the answers tell you more than a price list would.

02Questions about the food: who cooks and how is it made?

  1. Who will cook on the day? Ask whether the chef who plans the menu is the one who cooks it.
  2. Is the food cooked fresh or prepared elsewhere? Fresh on-site cooking changes the quality of hot dishes and canapés.
  3. Can the menu be tailored? A set menu is fine, but a good caterer adapts it to your guests and the season.

A chef-led team, such as the one at Alejandro Nicolau’s corporate catering service, plans the menu around the event and cooks it on site, so what you taste in the proposal is what arrives on the day.

03Questions about the team: who serves and how many?

  1. How many staff will there be? The proposal should name the number of chefs and service staff, not just say a team will attend.
  2. Are they experienced in events like yours? A boardroom lunch, a launch and a Christmas party each need different service.
  3. Who is the contact on the day? One person should be responsible for solving problems quietly.

04Questions about the building: access, kitchen and timings

Office and venue logistics are where corporate events most often slip. A caterer who has worked in towers, listed buildings and galleries will ask these questions before you do.

  1. How do you handle lift bookings, loading and security passes? Ask what they need from building management and by when.
  2. What do you need in the kitchen? A pantry, a hob or nothing at all: the caterer should say what equipment they bring.
  3. What are your set-up and clear-down times? Both should fit inside the building’s access window.

05Questions about diets and allergies

  1. How do you collect and manage dietary needs? Expect a clear process, with a list agreed ahead of the day.
  2. How do you manage allergens? Honest answers include that cross-contact cannot be fully excluded in a shared kitchen, and that severe allergies are discussed before booking.

Be wary of any caterer who promises an allergen-free kitchen without explaining how. For more on how this works, read private chef dietary requirements and allergies.

06Questions about the proposal: what is included and what changes?

  1. What is included in the proposal? Menu, staffing, equipment, glassware, service time and clearing should all be listed.
  2. What is not included? Furniture, venue hire and drinks are often separate; ask directly.
  3. What happens if numbers change? Find the date by which numbers must be final and what flexibility there is either side of it.

A caterer who answers these clearly and in writing is usually one who is comfortable being held to the plan.

07What are the warning signs when choosing a caterer?

  • Vague answers about who cooks and who serves.
  • No questions about your building or kitchen.
  • A menu that cannot be changed for diets or season.
  • Promises of certification or allergen-free service without detail.
  • No written proposal, or one that avoids listing exclusions.

If two or more of these appear, keep looking. A good caterer is usually a little over-prepared, not under.

08Why does the type of event change the questions?

A boardroom lunch for twelve, a launch for 150 and a Christmas party for 60 stretch a caterer in different ways, so the questions should shift with the event.

  • Small, senior groups need discretion, precise timing and food that works around a meeting. Ask how service is timed to the agenda.
  • Receptions and launches need volume and pace. Ask how many canapés a chef can produce on site and how trays are managed.
  • Seated dinners and parties need a plan for plating and clearing at the same time. Ask how many staff are allocated per table or per guest.

It is worth asking a caterer for an example of a similar event they have run and what they would do differently for yours.

09What should be in writing before you confirm?

Verbal assurances do not help on the day. Before confirming, make sure the proposal states the following in plain language.

  • The final menu, including vegetarian and dietary dishes.
  • The number of chefs and service staff, and their arrival and finish times.
  • The equipment and glassware the caterer will bring.
  • The building arrangements the caterer has agreed with your facilities team.
  • The date by which numbers must be confirmed.

Keep the proposal alongside your building access confirmation. If anything changes, ask for an updated version rather than agreeing by phone.

10How do you compare caterers fairly?

Ask each caterer the same twelve questions and put the answers side by side. Compare the proposals on what is included, not just on how they look.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I ask a corporate caterer before booking?

Ask who cooks and serves, whether food is cooked fresh on site, how building access is handled, how dietary needs and allergens are managed, what the proposal includes and excludes, and what happens if guest numbers change.

How far ahead should I book a corporate caterer in London?

For regular events, three to four weeks is usually enough, but busy periods such as December need earlier booking. Share your date and numbers as soon as they are fixed so the team can confirm availability.

Do corporate caterers need access to a kitchen?

Not always. A chef-led team can bring portable equipment and work from a small pantry or prep area. Tell the caterer what facilities exist and they will plan the menu around them.

Can a private chef act as a corporate caterer?

Yes. A private chef with a team can cook lunches, dinners and receptions for companies, with fresh on-site cooking and staff, which is often a closer fit than a large catering company for smaller events.

How are dietary needs handled at corporate events?

A good caterer collects requirements early, plans separate dishes for each need and explains how cross-contact is managed. Severe allergies should be discussed before booking because a shared kitchen cannot exclude cross-contact entirely.
