How to Host a Cocktail Party at Home: Food, Drinks, Timings

By Alejandro Nicolau, Private Chef·Published 7 Nov 2026·Updated 7 Nov 2026

Key takeaways

  • Two to three hours is ideal. Longer and energy drops unless the format changes.
  • Keep the cocktail list short. Three to five drinks, with one alcohol-free, keep service quick.
  • Plan four to eight canapés per guest. More if the party runs past two hours or replaces dinner.
  • Design the flow. Bar, food and seating should be in different spots so guests circulate.
  • Add a bartender after 20 guests. Cocktails are slow to make and hosts rarely have the time.

To host a cocktail party at home, plan a two to three hour window, a short menu of three to five cocktails, four to eight canapés per guest and about one drink per guest per hour after the first. Keep a clear flow for guests, decide who is making drinks and consider a bartender once you pass 20 guests.

Knowing how to host a cocktail party at home comes down to timing, a short menu and a clear flow. Alejandro Nicolau, a London private chef trained under Martin Berasategui, cooks and organises food and bar service for private cocktail parties at homes across London. This is the planning method the team uses, step by step.

01How long should a cocktail party at home last?

Two to three hours is the sweet spot. It gives guests time to arrive, mingle and eat without the evening drifting. Put the start and end times on the invitation; an open-ended party tends to lose its momentum.

A common pattern is 6.30pm to 9pm, which suits guests coming from work and finishes before most people need dinner.

02How many guests can you host at home?

A standing party needs roughly one to one and a half square metres per guest. A living room of 25 square metres comfortably holds around 15 to 20 people; a house with a connected kitchen and garden can take more.

If your space is limited, use two rooms or the garden, and keep chairs to a few spots so people circulate rather than settling.

03How do you plan the cocktail menu?

  1. Choose three to five cocktails, with one lighter, one stirred and one long drink.
  2. Add an alcohol-free serve, plus wine, beer and water.
  3. Batch what can be prepared ahead, such as syrups, juices and infusions.
  4. Finish drinks to order so they arrive fresh.

For more on building the list, see how to plan a cocktail menu for a private party and welcome cocktail ideas.

04What food should you serve at a cocktail party?

Canapés are the natural choice: small, tidy and eaten in one or two bites. Plan four to six per guest for a short party before dinner, and twelve to fifteen if the canapés are the meal.

  • A cold element, such as cured fish or a tartlet.
  • A hot element, such as croquetas or a mini skewer.
  • A vegetarian piece that is a proper dish, not a leftover.
  • Something sweet at the end.

See how many canapés per person for detailed figures and canapé catering for how a chef-led team handles it.

05How many drinks per person should you plan?

Plan two drinks per guest in the first hour and one per hour after that, with about ten per cent spare, plus alcohol-free options. See how many drinks per person for a party.

Cocktails are slower to make than wine, so a party of 30 on cocktails needs more bar capacity than a party of 30 on wine.

06What does the timeline look like?

  1. One week before: confirm guests, menu and who is serving.
  2. The day before: prepare syrups, infusions and prep, buy ice and clear space.
  3. Two hours before: set up the bar, glassware and food stations.
  4. Arrival: first drink in hand within minutes, first canapés shortly after.
  5. Mid-party: substantial canapés and a refresh of drinks and ice.
  6. Last hour: sweet pieces, coffee and a short farewell round.

07How do you design the space for a cocktail party?

Where things go affects how the party feels. A good layout spreads people through the space and avoids queues.

  • Put the bar away from the entrance and the kitchen, so guests move through the room to reach it.
  • Place canapé trays and tables in a second area, so food and drink are in different spots.
  • Provide a few high tables or ledges for glasses and plates.
  • Keep seating limited, in small groups.
  • Clear a route for staff to circulate with trays.

If you have a garden or terrace, use it as a second zone, weather allowing.

08What do you do about music, lighting and the arrival?

The first fifteen minutes set the tone. Guests should have a drink in hand soon after arriving and something to eat within a few minutes.

  • Keep music low enough for conversation and increase it gently as the evening goes on.
  • Dim the overhead lights and use lamps and candles for warmth.
  • Have a team member at the door to take coats and offer the first drink.
  • Prepare the first drink in advance so it can be served immediately.

These details cost little and make a home party feel like an event.

09When should you hire a bartender and chef?

Once you pass about 20 guests, hosting and mixing at the same time becomes hard. A bartender and a chef-led team let you spend the party with your guests rather than behind the bar or in the kitchen.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you host a cocktail party at home?

Plan two to three hours, a short menu of three to five cocktails, four to eight canapés per guest and one drink per guest per hour after the first two. Design the space for circulation and consider a bartender above 20 guests.

How many cocktails should you serve at a party?

Three to five is ideal, with one lighter, one stirred and one long drink plus an alcohol-free serve. A short list keeps service fast and the quality high.

How much food do you need for a cocktail party?

Four to six canapés per guest for a short party before dinner, six to eight for a longer one and twelve to fifteen if canapés are the meal. Add about ten per cent spare.

How long should a cocktail party last?

Two to three hours. Put a start and finish time on the invitation, such as 6.30pm to 9pm, so the party has momentum and guests know when to leave.

Can a private chef cater a cocktail party at home?

Yes. A chef and team can cook canapés on site, run the bar and handle service and clearing, so you can host without working during the party.
