How to Host a Dinner Party for 8: Menu and Timeline

By Alejandro Nicolau, Private Chef·Published 7 Nov 2026·Updated 7 Nov 2026
In this article

  4. How do you set the table for 8?
  5. How do you manage drinks and conversation?

  7. What menu ideas work well for a dinner party for 8?
  8. How do you cater for dietary needs at a dinner party?
  9. When should you hire a private chef for a dinner party?

Key takeaways

  • Choose one live course. Make the starter and dessert ahead so only the main is cooked on the night.
  • Work backwards from sitting down. A written timeline from shopping to serving prevents panic.
  • Eight is a good number. It fits one table and allows conversation across it.
  • Set the table early. Do it in the morning, with glasses, candles and name cards.
  • Ask about diets first. Collect needs with the invitation, not at the table.

To host a dinner party for 8, choose a three-course menu where only one course needs last-minute cooking, shop two days ahead, prepare the starter and dessert the day before and set the table in the morning. Allow 15 minutes for drinks on arrival, two and a half hours at the table and a calm ten minutes before guests arrive.

Learning how to host a dinner party for 8 is mostly about timing, which is why a written plan helps so much. Alejandro Nicolau, a London private chef trained under Martin Berasategui, cooks dinner parties for eight in homes across London every week. The method below is what makes the evening calm for the host, and it works whether you cook yourself or bring in a chef.

01How should you plan a dinner party menu for 8?

The principle is to have only one course that demands your attention on the night. The starter should be cold or finished in minutes, the main can be slow-cooked or roasted, and the dessert should be made the day before.

  • Starter: a chilled soup, a cured fish plate or a seasonal salad that can be assembled before guests arrive.
  • Main: a slow-cooked or oven dish, such as braised beef, roast chicken or baked fish, with vegetables that can share the oven.
  • Dessert: a tart, a set cream or a fruit dish made the day before and served at room temperature or cold.

Choose dishes you have cooked before. A dinner party is not the moment to learn a new technique.

02How much food do you need for 8 guests?

  • Starter: about 80 to 100 grams per person for solid components, or a small cup or bowl of soup.
  • Main protein: about 150 to 200 grams per person, boneless weight, and a little more if bone-in.
  • Vegetables and sides: about 250 grams in total per person across two or three dishes.
  • Dessert: a generous single portion, with a little extra for guests who ask for more.
  • Bread and nibbles: something small on arrival so nobody starts hungry.

Buy a small buffer, around ten per cent, so that nobody feels short and you have something for the next day.

03What is the timeline for a dinner party?

  1. One week before: confirm guests, collect dietary needs and choose the menu.
  2. Three days before: write the shopping list and order anything special, such as fish or meat.
  3. Two days before: shop for non-perishables and drinks; plan the table.
  4. The day before: prepare the dessert, any sauces and the starter components; chill wine and clean the space.
  5. The morning: set the table, arrange flowers and candles and put music on.
  6. Two hours before: put the main in the oven and prepare the starter plates.
  7. One hour before: shower and change; set out drinks and nibbles.
  8. Arrival: welcome drink in hand, starter ready to plate.

Write it down. A short timeline stuck to the fridge removes the need to remember anything on the night.

04How do you set the table for 8?

Eight around one table is intimate and sociable. A table of about 2.4 metres by 1 metre seats eight comfortably, with four on each long side, or six on the sides and one at each end.

  • Allow about 60 centimetres of table width per guest.
  • Set out cutlery from the outside in, in the order of the courses.
  • Use low flowers or candles so guests can see each other across the table.
  • Place name cards if there are guests who do not know each other.
  • Put water on the table from the start.

See hosting etiquette with a private chef at home for more on how to handle the host role.

05How do you manage drinks and conversation?

Pour a welcome drink as guests arrive, with something alcohol-free alongside it. Plan wine to match the courses, about half a bottle per guest across the meal, and keep water filled.

Seat people so that conversation flows: separate couples, mix interests and put the quieter guests next to someone warm. See wine and menu pairing for a private dinner for matching wine to the menu.

06What do you do on the night?

  • Greet guests, take coats and put a drink in each hand.
  • Serve the starter at the table, within 30 minutes of the first arrival.
  • Clear plates between courses without leaving the table for long.
  • Bring the main to the table for guests to serve themselves, or plate it in advance.
  • Leave the dessert until everyone has caught their breath.

If something goes wrong, laugh and carry on. Guests remember how they felt at the table, not a slightly overcooked vegetable.

07What menu ideas work well for a dinner party for 8?

Here are three simple menus that follow the one-live-course rule, one for each season.

  • Spring: pea and mint soup, roast lamb or baked sea bass with new potatoes and asparagus, and a lemon tart.
  • Autumn: roasted squash with burrata, slow-cooked beef with root vegetables and a baked pear dessert.
  • Winter: a chicory and orange salad, a braised chicken or a mushroom and chestnut pie, and a chocolate pot.

Each can be made largely in advance, with only the main needing attention in the hours before dinner.

08How do you cater for dietary needs at a dinner party?

Ask every guest about diets and allergies with the invitation, and again a week before. It is far easier to plan around requirements than to improvise at the table.

  • Choose a main that can be adapted, with sauce and sides served separately.
  • Make the vegetarian option as good as the main, not a leftover.
  • Label dishes that contain common allergens.
  • If a guest has a severe allergy, talk to them directly about what is safe.

A private chef will take care of this as part of planning and tell you honestly what they can and cannot guarantee.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you host a dinner party for 8?

Choose a three-course menu where only the main is cooked on the night, shop two days ahead, prepare the starter and dessert the day before and set the table in the morning. Collect dietary needs with the invitations.

How much food do you need for a dinner party of 8?

Plan roughly 80 to 100 grams of starter, 150 to 200 grams of boneless main protein and 250 grams of vegetables and sides per person, a generous dessert portion and a ten per cent buffer.

How long should a dinner party last?

A three-course dinner usually runs two and a half to three hours at the table, plus about 15 to 30 minutes for drinks on arrival. Coffee and conversation can run longer.

What should you cook for a dinner party?

Choose dishes you have cooked before and keep only one course for the night. A cold starter, a slow-cooked or oven main and a dessert made the day before is the most reliable pattern.

Can a private chef cook a dinner party for 8?

Yes. A chef can plan the menu with you, shop, cook and serve at your home and leave the kitchen clean, so you can sit and enjoy the evening with your guests.
