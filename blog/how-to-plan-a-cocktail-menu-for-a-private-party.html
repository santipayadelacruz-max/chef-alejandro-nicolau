How to Plan a Cocktail Menu for a Private Party

A great cocktail menu for a private event isn't about listing as many drinks as possible — it's about a small, well-balanced selection that suits your guests and keeps the bar moving smoothly all evening.

01Start with three to five signature drinks

A focused list of three to five cocktails, built around different spirit bases and flavour profiles, gives guests real choice without overwhelming the bar team or slowing down service with too many complex, one-off requests.

02Think about the occasion

A summer garden event calls for lighter, refreshing drinks; a winter dinner suits something warmer and more spirit-forward. Matching the menu to the season and setting makes the whole evening feel more considered.

03Balance classic and original

A menu built entirely around obscure, original cocktails can alienate guests who just want a good gin and tonic or an Old Fashioned. Mixing a couple of elevated classics with one or two original, signature creations tends to work best.

04Don't forget non-alcoholic options

A well-considered cocktail menu includes at least one or two proper non-alcoholic options, made with the same care as the alcoholic drinks — not an afterthought squeezed in at the last minute.

05Build it around fresh ingredients

House-made syrups, fresh juices and proper garnishes are what separate a genuinely good cocktail menu from a generic one. This is the same standard applied across every event on the Bar page.

06Let the bar team guide you

An experienced bar team can suggest a menu based on your guest profile and event style, rather than leaving you to build a list from scratch — this collaboration usually produces a far stronger result.

07Testing the Menu Before the Big Night

For significant events, it's worth asking whether a small tasting of the proposed cocktail menu can be arranged in advance — much like a food tasting for a wedding. This gives you the chance to adjust sweetness, strength, or specific flavour combinations before the actual event, rather than discovering a preference mismatch in front of your guests. Not every event calls for this level of preparation, but for a wedding or milestone celebration, it's a worthwhile extra step.

08Naming Drinks to Add a Personal Touch

Giving a signature cocktail a personal name — tied to the couple, the birthday person, or the occasion itself — is a small but memorable detail that guests often comment on well after the event, and it costs nothing extra beyond a little creative thought.

09A Menu Worth Getting Right

A well-planned cocktail menu is one of the more affordable ways to elevate a private event significantly — a small amount of extra thought here goes a long way toward how memorable the evening feels.

10Considering Garnish and Presentation Too

Beyond the drink itself, garnish and glassware contribute meaningfully to how a cocktail menu is experienced — a beautifully garnished drink photographs well and adds to the overall sense of occasion, which matters for events where guests will remember and share the evening afterwards.

11One Last Consideration: Ice

Good quality, ample ice is an easy detail to overlook but makes a genuine difference to both the taste and presentation of cocktails — worth explicitly confirming as part of your bar planning.

12Keeping the menu practical

Choose cocktails that share ingredients, so the bar is not carrying dozens of different bottles, and balance the list with a light option, a spirit-forward option and an alcohol-free serve. Consider glassware, ice and garnish, because these affect speed of service. Offer a clear description for each drink, so guests can choose quickly. A short, well-edited menu is easier for bartenders to execute at a high standard all night.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How many cocktails should be on the menu for a wedding?

Three to five well-chosen cocktails usually works best for a wedding, giving enough variety without overcomplicating service on an already busy night.

Should the cocktail menu match the food menu?

It is worth considering, especially for a themed event. A Spanish-inspired dinner, for example, pairs naturally with sherry-based or Spanish-spirit cocktails.
