Private Chef for an Anniversary Dinner: Romantic Menu Ideas

By Alejandro Nicolau, Private Chef·Published 1 Oct 2026·Updated 11 Oct 2026

An anniversary dinner at home, done well, can feel more special than a restaurant booking — no other diners, no rushed table turnaround, just an evening built entirely around the two of you.

Keep the menu intimate, not overwhelming

A shorter, more considered tasting menu — four to six courses — tends to suit an anniversary dinner better than an exhaustive multi-course marathon, keeping the focus on connection rather than an endless parade of plates.

Revisit something meaningful

A dish tied to how you met, a cuisine from a memorable trip together, or a flavour combination that means something specific to your relationship adds a personal layer that elevates the evening beyond just "a nice dinner."

Consider the atmosphere as much as the food

Lighting, table setting, and pacing all matter for a romantic evening — a private chef can time courses to allow for a slower, more relaxed rhythm than a typical dinner party.

Pair it with something special to drink

A considered cocktail or wine pairing, perhaps built around a drink from your first date or wedding, adds another layer of personal meaning to the evening.

Let someone else handle the details

The point of an anniversary dinner is being fully present with each other — a private chef manages the cooking, plating and clean-up, so neither of you spends the evening in the kitchen instead of at the table together.

Making Space for Conversation, Not Just Courses

The best anniversary menus are designed with enough breathing room between courses for genuine conversation, rather than a tightly paced sequence that keeps interrupting the evening. Discussing this pacing preference with your chef in advance — a slower, more relaxed rhythm rather than an efficient, restaurant-style service — helps ensure the evening feels like quality time together, not just a well-executed meal.

A Nice Touch for Milestone Anniversaries Specifically

For a significant milestone — a 10th, 25th or 50th anniversary — revisiting the actual menu from your wedding day, reimagined slightly, can be a particularly meaningful way to mark just how far you've come together.

Celebrating What Matters

An anniversary dinner built with real thought and personal meaning is a lovely way to mark another year together — proof that some of the best celebrations happen quietly, at your own table.

Considering the Setting Alongside the Menu

Beyond the food itself, small touches to the setting — candles, flowers, a carefully chosen playlist — work alongside a well-planned menu to create a genuinely romantic atmosphere, and are worth discussing as part of the overall evening rather than left as an afterthought.

A Final Thought

Sometimes the most romantic gesture isn't the most elaborate one — a thoughtfully chosen, genuinely personal menu often means more than an overly extravagant evening ever could.

Quality Time, Well Served

At its heart, an anniversary dinner is about protected, undistracted time together — everything else, including the menu, exists to support that.

In Short

A well-planned anniversary dinner isn't about impressing anyone else — it's entirely, deliberately, just for the two of you.

Making the evening feel personal

The details that people remember are usually small ones: the dish you ate on your first holiday together, the wine from the year you met, a dessert that echoes a wedding cake. Share these when you enquire and the private chef can weave them into the menu without making a fuss. Smaller touches, such as a handwritten menu card, candlelight on the table and a quiet pace between courses, turn a good dinner into one that feels designed for the two of you.
