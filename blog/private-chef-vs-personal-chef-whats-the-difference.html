Private Chef vs Personal Chef: What's the Difference?

"Private chef" and "personal chef" get used interchangeably in everyday conversation, but within the industry they typically describe two genuinely different working arrangements.

01What a personal chef typically means

A personal chef usually works on an ongoing, regular basis for one household — planning and preparing meals across the week, often as a recurring arrangement rather than a one-off booking. It's closer to a long-term service relationship.

02What a private chef typically means

A private chef is more commonly booked for specific events or occasions — a dinner party, a celebration, a corporate event, or a yacht charter — bringing a tailored menu and full service for that particular occasion, without an ongoing weekly commitment.

03Where the lines blur

In practice, many chefs — including this service — offer both models: one-off event bookings across tasting menus and celebrations, alongside an ongoing meal prep service that functions more like the personal chef model.

04Which one you actually need

If you're planning a specific event — a birthday, an anniversary, a corporate dinner — you're looking for private chef-style event service. If you want regular, ongoing meals prepared for your household across the week, that's closer to the personal chef model.

05Why the label matters less than the fit

Ultimately, the terminology matters less than finding a chef whose style, experience and approach suit what you actually need — whether that's a single spectacular evening or a weekly rhythm of well-planned meals.

06A Real Example of How This Plays Out

Consider a client who books a private chef for a single milestone birthday, enjoys the experience, and later decides they'd like similarly good food every week without the effort of cooking. That client naturally transitions from the private chef, event-based model into something closer to the personal chef, ongoing model — which is exactly why many chefs, this service included, are set up to support both relationships rather than forcing a choice between them.

07Asking Directly About Availability for Both Models

If you think you might want both an event booking now and an ongoing arrangement later, it's worth asking upfront whether a chef offers both models. Not every private chef also runs an ongoing meal prep service, so clarifying this early avoids having to search separately later.

08Choosing What Actually Fits

Whichever model suits your situation, the important thing is finding a chef whose experience and style genuinely match what you're looking for, rather than getting caught up in the terminology itself.

09A Question Worth Asking Directly

If you're unsure which arrangement best fits your needs, simply asking a prospective chef directly how they typically structure client relationships — one-off events, ongoing arrangements, or both — is the fastest way to get clarity before booking anything.

10A Final Thought

Whichever term you use, what actually matters is finding someone whose food, communication and reliability genuinely fit what you need — the label is simply a useful shorthand, not the deciding factor.

11Focus on Fit, Not Labels

The right working relationship is the one that fits your actual rhythm of life — whatever it ends up being called.

12Which model suits your household

If you entertain occasionally and want a restaurant-level evening on specific dates, an event-based private chef is usually the better fit. If you want regular, healthy meals through the week with little effort, an ongoing personal chef or meal prep arrangement makes more sense. Some households use both, with weekly prepared meals for everyday life and a private chef booking for celebrations. The right choice depends less on the label and more on how often you want help in the kitchen.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the same chef offer both event bookings and ongoing meal prep?

Yes. Many chefs, including Alejandro Nicolau's service, offer both one-off event bookings and ongoing meal prep, so you can move between the two as your needs change.

Is a personal chef more expensive than booking a private chef per event?

It depends on frequency. An ongoing personal chef arrangement is typically priced differently from a single private chef event booking because the scope of work is different.
