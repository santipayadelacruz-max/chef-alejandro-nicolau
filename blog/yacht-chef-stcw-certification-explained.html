STCW Certification and What It Means for Your Yacht Chef

By Alejandro Nicolau, Private Chef·Published 1 Oct 2026·Updated 11 Oct 2026

If you're booking a chef for a yacht charter, one credential is worth understanding before anything else: STCW certification. It's not a nice-to-have — it's the baseline safety qualification for anyone working on board.

What STCW actually is

STCW stands for Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping — an international framework for training anyone who works at sea. The Basic Safety Training component covers fire prevention and firefighting, elementary first aid, personal survival techniques, and personal safety and social responsibility on board.

Why it matters for a yacht chef specifically

A galley is one of the higher-risk areas of a yacht — open flames, hot oil, and sharp equipment, all in a moving vessel. STCW training means a chef understands how to work safely in that environment and how to respond correctly if something goes wrong, for their own safety and everyone else's on board.

It also reflects professionalism

Holding STCW certification signals that a chef has committed to yacht work as a serious discipline, not an extension of land-based catering. It's typically expected by yacht captains and management companies before a chef is even considered for a charter.

What to ask before booking a yacht chef

It's entirely reasonable to ask directly whether a prospective yacht chef holds current STCW certification, alongside their experience cooking specifically in galley environments — the two go hand in hand for a smooth, safe charter.

Beyond the certificate

Certification is the baseline, but real galley experience — understanding provisioning logistics, working efficiently in small spaces, and adapting menus around a charter's itinerary — is what actually determines the quality of the food and service you'll experience on board.

What Happens Without Proper Certification

Booking an uncertified chef for yacht work isn't just a compliance issue — it's a genuine safety risk in an environment where fire, confined spaces and constant movement all raise the stakes of any kitchen accident. A chef without STCW training may be an excellent cook on land, but may not know the correct response to a galley fire at sea, or how to move safely during rough conditions. This is precisely why captains and charter management companies treat the certification as a non-negotiable baseline, not a nice-to-have.

How to Verify Certification Before Booking

It's entirely reasonable to ask for evidence of current STCW certification directly, the same way you might ask for any other professional credential. A chef who has genuinely completed the training will have no hesitation sharing this, and it's a simple, low-friction way to add confidence before finalising a yacht chef booking.

A Simple Standard to Look For

When comparing yacht chefs for a charter, treat STCW certification as a baseline requirement, not a bonus — it's the clearest, simplest way to ensure your chosen chef is properly prepared for the realities of working at sea.

A Small Detail With Real Consequences

Certification might seem like a small administrative detail compared to the excitement of planning a charter menu, but it's precisely the kind of unglamorous groundwork that ensures everything else about the trip can go smoothly and safely.

A Final Word

When so much of a successful charter depends on trust in the people running it, a properly certified, experienced yacht chef is one of the simplest ways to start that trust on solid ground.

Why owners and captains ask for it

STCW training ensures that every crew member can respond calmly in an emergency, from fires to evacuation. Owners and captains value this as a standard of professionalism, and insurers often expect it. For guests, it is reassurance that the person cooking in the galley also understands the safety culture on board. Keeping certificates current is part of being a serious yacht chef.
