The details

What to expect

01What halal-friendly means here

Halal-friendly means the menu is built around halal principles: no pork or pork products, no alcohol used in cooking, and meat bought from a halal supplier when that is what you want. The details differ between families, so each booking begins with a conversation about what you observe and how strictly. Everything is written clearly into the proposal, so there are no assumptions and nobody at the table has to ask what is in a dish.

02Sourcing you can rely on

When the menu includes meat, it can be ordered from a halal butcher of your choice or one recommended to you. Many families have a trusted supplier, and using that supplier is always welcome. Ingredients are checked for hidden pork derivatives and alcohol, such as gelatine, certain stocks, vanilla extracts and sauces, so that the food is right from the first ingredient to the last garnish.

03Cooking without alcohol

Classical sauces often depend on wine, brandy or sherry, but great flavour does not have to. Menus use stocks, citrus, verjuice, vinegars, herbs, spices and slow reductions to build depth, so dishes taste rich and layered rather than simplified. Desserts are made without alcohol too, using vanilla pods, fruit, dark chocolate and nuts in place of liqueurs. The result is food that feels complete, not food with something missing.

04Eid, Ramadan and family celebrations

Iftar dinners, Eid gatherings, engagement parties, weddings and large family meals all have their own rhythm. Dates, soups, light starters and generous sharing dishes can open an iftar, followed by a full menu when the family is ready. For Eid, the table is often large and the cooking abundant, and the team can support larger guest lists with service and clearing up, so the hosts can sit down with their guests.

05Flavours from many kitchens

Halal-friendly menus can draw on any cuisine that suits the occasion: Middle Eastern mezze and slow-cooked lamb, Mediterranean seafood, Indian and Pakistani spice, North African tagines, Spanish and Italian dishes adapted without pork or wine, or an elegant fine-dining tasting menu. You choose the direction, and the menu is shaped around the style your guests will enjoy most.

06Dietary needs on top of halal

Many tables have additional needs beyond halal, such as nut allergies, gluten intolerance, vegetarian guests or children with their own preferences. These are planned into the same menu from the start, and each guest's requirements are noted on the proposal. Having it all in one place avoids the stress of managing separate dishes on the night.

07Private dining across London

Dinners are cooked in homes throughout London, including Mayfair, Kensington, Knightsbridge, Marylebone, Hampstead, Wembley and Harrow, and for events elsewhere in the United Kingdom and abroad. Alejandro handles the shopping, cooking, service and clearing up, so the host can focus on the guests rather than the kitchen.

Questions

Halal Private Chef London: FAQs

Is the food halal certified?

The cooking is halal-friendly: no pork, no alcohol in the cooking, and halal-sourced meat when requested. Formal certification of the cooking itself is not offered, so if you need that, say so at the start.

Where does the meat come from?

From a halal butcher. You can name your own trusted supplier or ask for a recommendation, and it is written into the proposal.

Do you use alcohol in the cooking?

No, not in a halal-friendly menu. Flavour is built with stocks, citrus, vinegars, herbs and spices, and desserts are made without liqueurs.

Can you cook an iftar or an Eid dinner?

Yes. Tell us the number of guests, the timing and the dishes that matter to your family, and the menu is planned around them.

Can you also cater for allergies and other diets?

Yes. Allergies, vegetarian guests and other requirements are planned into the same menu from the first conversation.
