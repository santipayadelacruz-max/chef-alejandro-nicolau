Regional Italy, cooked properly

Good Italian cooking is deceptively simple to describe and genuinely difficult to do well — a handful of ingredients, properly sourced, treated with restraint rather than piled on top of each other. Alejandro's Italian menus draw on classic regional technique, from fresh handmade pasta to a properly reduced ragù, built around the same seasonal, ingredient-led approach that runs through his wider menus, cooked and served entirely in your own home.

What to expect

01Regional, not generic

Italian cooking varies enormously by region, and menus here reflect that rather than flattening everything into one generic "Italian" style — a lighter, seafood-forward approach drawing on coastal traditions, or a richer, more rustic menu with roots further inland, chosen to suit the occasion and your own preferences.

02Handmade pasta, done properly

Fresh pasta made and shaped on the day is a centrepiece of many Italian bookings, whether that's a simple, perfectly cooked tagliatelle or a more involved filled pasta course — made from scratch rather than a shortcut, exactly as a proper Italian kitchen would do it.

03Pairing with wine

An Italian menu pairs naturally with a wine-led evening, and where a client wants a specific pairing alongside each course, this is planned as part of the same booking, whether drawing on your own cellar or a recommendation discussed during planning.

04What's included

A booking includes a full planning conversation to settle the menu and any regional preference, all shopping and ingredient sourcing — including good olive oil, proper Italian cheeses and cured meats where called for — cooking on-site in your own kitchen, plated or family-style service, and a complete clean-up before the team leaves.

05Suited to any occasion

An Italian menu works equally well for a relaxed dinner party among friends and a more formal celebration, scaled from an intimate dinner for two through to a larger family gathering, with the format discussed as part of the initial consultation.

06Pairing with the rest of the menu

An Italian course or two can also be worked into a broader, multi-cuisine tasting menu where a client wants variety across the evening rather than committing fully to one tradition — discussed as part of the initial planning conversation.

07Sourcing proper ingredients

Good Italian cooking depends on good ingredients, and sourcing for a booking here reflects that — proper DOP cheeses, good cured meats and genuinely ripe produce rather than a generic substitute.

08Dessert, done the proper Italian way

A proper Italian menu ends with dessert given the same care as everything before it — a genuinely good tiramisu made fresh, or a seasonal fruit-based dessert, rather than a generic finish unrelated to the rest of the meal.

09Booking an Italian menu

An enquiry through the contact form, with a sense of your date, guest count and any regional preference, is all that's needed to start planning — a full proposal follows within 24 hours.

10Wine to match

A classic Italian menu is built with wine pairing in mind from the outset — a crisp Northern white alongside early courses, moving toward a fuller-bodied red as richer pasta and meat courses arrive. Alejandro is happy to suggest a pairing structure for your own cellar or a wine merchant to work with.

11How it's presented

Plating stays close to the Italian tradition itself: generous, unfussy portions that let a handful of excellent ingredients speak for themselves, rather than elaborate garnish for its own sake.

12A typical Italian dinner, course by course

An Italian evening usually opens with antipasti to share, such as burrata with seasonal tomatoes, cured meats and marinated vegetables, followed by a pasta course made by hand that day. A main of fish or meat comes next, kept simple and well seasoned, with a side of vegetables cooked properly. The meal closes with a classic dessert such as panna cotta, tiramisu or a fruit tart. Courses arrive at a relaxed pace, so guests can talk between plates rather than feel rushed.

13Ingredients that make the difference

Italian cooking depends on ingredient quality more than almost any other cuisine, so sourcing matters. Menus are built around good extra virgin olive oil, aged Parmigiano Reggiano, ripe seasonal produce and fish bought fresh from a trusted supplier. When something is out of season, the menu changes rather than compromises. Tell Alejandro about your favourite regions and dishes and he will build the evening around what is best on the day you book.

Italian Private Chef: FAQs

Is the pasta made fresh on the day, or pre-made?

Yes. On an Italian booking, Alejandro Nicolau makes and shapes the pasta fresh on the day as part of the service, rather than using pre-made or shortcut pasta.

Can the menu focus on a specific region of Italy?

Yes. Alejandro Nicolau's Italian menus can lean toward a specific region's traditions, which is agreed with you during the initial planning conversation.

Is a vegetarian Italian menu available?

Yes. A vegetarian Italian menu from Alejandro Nicolau keeps the full character of the cuisine, built around seasonal vegetables and good pasta.

Can an Italian menu be paired with wine from my own cellar?

Yes. An Italian menu from Alejandro Nicolau can be paired with wine from your own cellar, or he can suggest a pairing structure as part of the planning.
