Thai Private Chef

Sweet, sour, salty, spicy — balanced

As Featured In

Good Thai cooking lives or dies on balance — the interplay of sweet, sour, salty and spicy that defines the cuisine, easy to describe and genuinely hard to get right consistently. Alejandro's Thai menus are built around that same balance, using properly sourced aromatics and fresh herbs rather than a simplified, pre-mixed approach, giving guests the real thing rather than a Westernised version.

The details

What to expect

01Fresh aromatics, not shortcuts

Lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime and fresh chilli are sourced and prepared properly for each booking, rather than relying on pre-made pastes — the difference is noticeable in the final balance of each dish.

02A menu that moves through the country

Thai regional cooking varies considerably, from richer, coconut-led southern curries to the drier, herb-forward cooking of the north and northeast, and a tasting menu can move through several of these styles across one evening.

03Heat, properly judged

Spice level is discussed as part of planning, so a Thai menu can run from a genuinely fiery regional profile through to a gentler, more approachable version, without losing the dish's underlying balance either way.

04What's included

A Thai booking includes menu planning around regional style and spice preference, all shopping and sourcing of fresh aromatics, on-site cooking and service, and a complete clean-up before the team leaves.

05Suited to a warm-weather event

Thai cuisine's bright, fresh flavours make it a particularly popular choice for a summer garden event, though it works equally well as an indoor dinner at any time of year.

06Fresh herbs, prepared on the day

Thai cooking depends heavily on fresh herbs — Thai basil, coriander, mint — prepared and added at exactly the right moment, something that's planned carefully as part of the booking rather than left to a pre-made base.

07A menu that balances richness and freshness

A well-planned Thai tasting menu moves between richer curries and lighter, fresher dishes across the evening, giving the whole meal a sense of rhythm rather than a single unvaried register.

08A sweet, refreshing finish

Thai desserts tend toward something light and refreshing after a meal built on bold, balanced flavour — fresh tropical fruit, coconut-based sweets or a simple sticky rice dessert, chosen to close the evening on the right note.

09Booking a Thai menu

An enquiry through the contact form, with your date, guest count and any spice preference, starts the planning process, with a full proposal confirmed within 24 hours.

10Scaling to your event

A Thai menu is planned to suit any guest count, from a quiet dinner for two through to a larger garden party, with the kitchen team scaled to match the scope of the event.

11Equipment & setup

The team brings any specialist equipment a Thai menu calls for, including a proper wok setup, so a standard home kitchen is all that's needed from the host.

12Suited to a smaller gathering too

A Thai menu works just as well for an intimate dinner for two as for a larger garden party, with the number of dishes scaled to match your specific guest count.

13Drinks to match

A crisp, slightly off-dry white or a light lager generally works best against Thai food's balance of heat, sourness and sweetness, and Alejandro is glad to suggest pairings suited to the specific dishes chosen for your menu.

14How it's presented

Herbs and aromatics are added at the very last moment before serving, so each plate reaches the table with the fresh, fragrant lift that a Thai dish loses quickly once it's been sitting.

15Balance is the heart of Thai cooking

Thai food depends on balancing sweet, sour, salty, bitter and spicy in every dish, and on fresh herbs used with a generous hand. Curry pastes are made from scratch with lemongrass, galangal, chilli and kaffir lime, rather than from jars. The result is bright, layered food that tastes alive, and the heat can be set to suit the table, with extra chilli served on the side for those who like it.

16Sharing dishes, family style

Thai dinners work best when dishes arrive together and are shared across the table, with rice at the centre. A typical menu might include a fresh salad, grilled skewers, a curry, a stir-fried dish and something steamed, finishing with a cool, fruity dessert. This relaxed, sharing format suits dinner parties, family occasions and casual celebrations, and it gives guests the chance to try a little of everything.

Questions

Thai Private Chef: FAQs

Is the Thai menu adjusted for guests who don't want a lot of heat?

Yes. Alejandro Nicolau plans the heat of a Thai menu with you during the initial consultation and calibrates it to your table without compromising the dish's overall balance.

Are fresh Thai ingredients, like lemongrass and galangal, used rather than shortcuts?

Yes. On a Thai booking, Alejandro Nicolau sources and prepares fresh aromatics such as lemongrass and galangal specifically for your event, rather than relying on pre-made pastes.

Can the menu include dishes from a specific region of Thailand?

Yes. A Thai menu from Alejandro Nicolau can focus on a specific region, such as the richer southern curries or the herb-forward cooking of the north.

Is a vegetarian or vegan Thai menu available?

Yes. Thai cuisine adapts well to vegetarian and vegan menus, and Alejandro Nicolau keeps the full depth of flavour in those versions.
